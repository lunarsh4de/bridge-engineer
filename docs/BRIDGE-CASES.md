# Real bridge cases and photographs — v1.4.0

Checked 2026-11-21. Local files work offline. Full original-image metadata is in `assets/bridges/SOURCES.json`. Photos are not AI-generated. Credits and licenses remain visible in every expanded case card. Wikimedia thumbnails have no local pixel edits. CC BY-SA applies to the respective photos, not the entire game.

Bridge facts were cross-checked against the linked primary sources where accessible, and the individual encyclopedia bridge articles and their references. Failed/removed official URLs were replaced with accessible specific sources; research logs are in the development output folder. No real navigation clearance, capacity or safety standard is claimed for the game.

## tabletop — Bollman Truss Railroad Bridge / 博尔曼铁路桁架桥

Follow the visible iron members from the deck to the supports. Different members can carry pull and push.

Model difference: The real bridge uses a patented iron suspension-truss system. Your small timber model shares the idea of load paths, not its material or exact layout.

Bridge source: https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/72000582_text

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bollman-bridge-1.jpg · William E. Barrett · Public domain · https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bollman-bridge-1.jpg

## willow — Cornish–Windsor Covered Bridge / 科尼什—温莎廊桥

Look through the timber lattice: many short diagonals form a braced pattern instead of one long unsupported member.

Model difference: The real bridge uses Town lattice timber trusses and has a roof. The game uses ideal pinned bars and leaves out the roof and detailed joints.

Bridge source: https://www.asce.org/about-civil-engineering/history-and-heritage/historic-landmarks/cornish-windsor-covered-bridge

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cornish-Windsor_Covered_Bridge,_Vermont.jpg · Ben Miller · CC BY-SA 2.0 · https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0

## clinic — Queensferry Crossing / 昆斯费里大桥

Direct stays run from the towers to the deck. The cables pull; towers and other members carry the balancing forces.

Model difference: This is a cable-stayed bridge. The game replaces its towers with fixed high anchors and omits the real deck’s bending behavior.

Bridge source: https://www.theforthbridges.org/about-the-forth-bridges/queensferry-crossing/

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Queensferry_Crossing_-_geograph.org.uk_-_5454314.jpg · John Allan · CC BY-SA 2.0 · https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0

## mill — Forth Bridge / 福斯铁路桥

The large steel cantilevers use braced members to carry railway loads. Member demand varies with position and structural role.

Model difference: The real bridge is a steel cantilever railway bridge. Your mixed timber-and-steel truss is a material-allocation exercise, not a replica.

Bridge source: https://www.theforthbridges.org/about-the-forth-bridges/forth-bridge/

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Forthrailbridgefromsouthqueensferry.jpg · George Gastin · CC BY-SA 3.0 · https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0

## echo — Millau Viaduct / 米约高架桥

The multi-pylon cable-stayed deck shows one way to cross a deep valley. Terrain and available supports influence the structural choice.

Model difference: The real viaduct has seven pylons. The game offers simplified truss or stay alternatives and does not reproduce its scale or foundations.

Bridge source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millau_Viaduct

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Creissels_et_Viaduct_de_Millau.jpg · Stefan Krause, Germany · CC BY-SA 3.0 · https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0

## finale — Rio–Antirrio Bridge / 里翁—安蒂里翁大桥

Several pylons divide a long crossing into supported spans. Look at how each deck segment connects to a tower through stays.

Model difference: The real crossing uses four pylons and a continuous cable-stayed deck. Your selectable pier, tower or truss support is a simplified comparison.

Bridge source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio%E2%80%93Antirrio_Bridge

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rio%E2%80%93Antirrio_Bridge_seen_from_the_road_below_Klokova_mountain_-_DSC09535.jpg · Bekuletz · Public domain · https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rio%E2%80%93Antirrio_Bridge_seen_from_the_road_below_Klokova_mountain_-_DSC09535.jpg

## convoy — Golden Gate Bridge / 金门大桥

Many vehicles can occupy a road bridge together. Their positions and spacing matter when engineers consider traffic loading.

Model difference: The real bridge is a suspension bridge with six roadway lanes. The game tests two prescribed trucks on one shared load path, not real traffic capacity.

Bridge source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gate_Bridge

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Golden_Gate_Bridge_as_seen_from_Marshall%E2%80%99s_Beach,_March_2018.jpg · Frank Schulenburg · CC BY-SA 4.0 · https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0

## harbor — Sydney Harbour Bridge / 悉尼海港大桥

The high arch carries the deck above a working harbor. Water traffic needs a clear route as well as road traffic needing support.

Model difference: The real bridge is a steel arch. Its actual navigation limits depend on the vessel and water level; the blue game envelope is an invented test condition.

Bridge source: https://www.sydney.com/destinations/sydney/sydney-city/sydney-harbour/sydney-harbour-bridge

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sydney_Harbour_Bridge_from_Circular_Quay.jpg · JJ Harrison (https://www.jjharrison.com.au/) · CC BY-SA 3.0 · https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0

## island — San Francisco–Oakland Bay Bridge / 旧金山—奥克兰海湾大桥

Yerba Buena Island links the western and eastern bridge sections. A crossing can be planned as connected parts with different support conditions.

Model difference: This historical aerial photo shows the 1936 bridge. The eastern span was replaced in 2013. The game’s two short spans and solid island omit the real tunnel and differing bridge systems.

Bridge source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco%E2%80%93Oakland_Bay_Bridge

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aerial_View_Of_Suspension_Bridge_To_Yerba_Buena_Island_And_Beyond_From_San_Francisco.jpg · Clyde Sunderland · Public domain · https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aerial_View_Of_Suspension_Bridge_To_Yerba_Buena_Island_And_Beyond_From_San_Francisco.jpg

## shipping — Øresund Bridge / 厄勒海峡大桥

The high bridge carries road and rail while preserving a shipping route beneath its main span. Traffic and navigation constraints must work together.

Model difference: The full Øresund link includes a bridge, artificial island and tunnel. The game tests only road trucks and a boat envelope, not railway or tunnel design.

Bridge source: https://www.oresundsbron.com/en/about-oresundsbron/about-us/facts-about-oresundsbron

Photo: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%C3%96resundsbron_October_2017_02.jpg · Arild Vågen · CC BY-SA 4.0 · https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0

## riverport — Rio–Antirrio Bridge / 里翁—安蒂里翁大桥

The bridge has navigation space between supported spans. A long crossing combines traffic demands, support layout and room for ships.

Model difference: The real bridge has four pylons in the gulf, not the game’s central island. Your final commission combines the constraints you tested earlier at a small fictional scale.

Bridge source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio%E2%80%93Antirrio_Bridge
